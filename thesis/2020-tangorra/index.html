Abstract

Managing parking spaces is one of the major challenges in urban mobility. The use of restricted hours or metered parking are the traditional ways in which municipalities have addressed this issue. However, finding a parking spot is becoming an increasingly difficult problem due to the continuous growth of the vehicle fleet.

In addition to drivers having to spend their time searching for parking, at least three negative side effects are generated:

  1. Air pollution, which harms the environment,
  2. Noise pollution, which bothers and affects citizens, and
  3. Traffic congestion, which impacts traffic flow and causes frustration among drivers.

The development of new technologies such as the Internet of Things, mobile devices, and smart parking meters is providing new tools to offer more services to citizens and to allow for more detailed control over the use of parking spaces.

Most existing works addressing the parking problem analyze it in the context of large cities, using various expensive devices, or by allowing users to participate in the collection of data that will later be used to provide possible solutions. This becomes even more difficult to implement in medium or small cities, where budget constraints are common, and the relatively small number of users can only provide a limited amount of data.

This work proposes the development of a smart parking solution primarily based on data obtained from the parking meters of the city of Tandil, a city where no solution to this problem has yet been implemented.

Abstract

Defining an argument as invalid is often easier than explaining why it is invalid—and even when an argument is refuted, the reasoning behind the refutation is often not sufficiently strong.

The main objective of this work is to develop an approach that, given an argumentative sentence expressed in natural language, can identify the flaws that render it invalid. This approach will provide the necessary support to detect argumentative errors in large volumes of data efficiently and with good performance.

To deepen the analysis, a semantic study of argumentative sentences will be carried out. Integrating semantics with syntax is not a trivial task, as it requires adapting ‘common sense’ for machines.

To conduct this study, a Knowledge Discovery in Databases (KDD) process is proposed, implementing each of its stages.

Ultimately, two tools will be developed with the goal of classifying sentences by detecting errors in their construction. To achieve this classification, various algorithms will be used to determine which one has the highest accuracy rate.

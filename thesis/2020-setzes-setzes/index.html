Abstract

The objective of this project was to develop an application for Android mobile phones capable of identifying a user’s relevant locations based on the traces left during their daily mobility. In addition, the application was able to recognize when the user returned to a previously visited location. For the development of the application, the aim was to find a solution that would be both as accurate and efficient as possible. To achieve this, the goal was to accurately detect the time the user spent at a specific location while minimizing mobile battery consumption. To this end, an algorithm was proposed that used WiFi network sensors, as they offer the best balance between accuracy and battery usage.

The learning algorithm was defined as performing the task of taking, as input, a s|equence of records obtained from the mobile device’s WiFi sensor and, based on them, producing a list of the places visited by the device. A specific sequence of records collected during a visit was used to define a waypoint for the location—essentially the “signature” stored for that place. This signature was then compared with other signatures obtained from subsequent visits to the same or different places. On the other hand, the recognition algorithm used the various waypoints learned during the previous stage—one for each location—to detect when the device returned to any of those places.

Once the application development was completed, an evaluation was carried out to validate the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed solution. During the evaluation, the application was installed on the mobile devices of a selected group of individuals with different daily routines, in order to ensure a good variety of visited locations. The volunteers were asked to record their entry and exit times for each place visited in a logbook. At the end of the experiment, the places detected by the developed application were compared against the ground truth obtained from the users’ manual entries.
